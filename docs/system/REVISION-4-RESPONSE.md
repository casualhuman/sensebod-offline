| **OWNER-4** | **Offline override code** ("like an authenticator app … but with longer ttl") | **Challenge-response, not TOTP** (ADR-017; reasons: the bag's clock can be wrong; TOTP codes are neither single-use nor bound to a request). **Codes:** the request code is 13 base32 characters (key version, bag tag, 40-bit nonce); the override code is 10 base32 characters (**50 bits**) from an HMAC with the per-bag secret over the bag id, the nonce and the username. **Expiry without a trusted clock:** a request expires at the first of 72 h of accumulated bag uptime (persisted across reboots, at most 5 min lost per power cut), 20 boots, or 72 h of wall clock only if the clock was trusted at both ends; also on use, after 5 wrong codes, or when the database is replaced. **Limits:** 3 new requests per 24 h of uptime, 3 open at once, and a pause after 15 failures. Request creation does not reveal whether an account exists. Guessing chance is about **1.3 × 10^-14 per day**. **Secret:** `K_bag` = HMAC(`M_v`, bag_id). It is stored in `keys/override.key` (`0600`), not in backups or logs. `M_v` exists only on EasySTEM's offline office machine, separate from the backup, release, content and registry keys. The registry holds only `key_version` and `revoked`. **Stolen card:** the thief can mint codes for that bag only, never for others (one-way derivation); EasySTEM then revokes the bag. **Rotation:** a versioned master; bags are rekeyed at re-provisioning or over the online path. **Tool:** `sensebod-office override` checks the bag tag and `revoked`, and logs every code it mints. **On success:** a restricted session that only allows a password change; all other sessions end; audited. **UI states** and **endpoints** are defined.
